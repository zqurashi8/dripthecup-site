How graphics cards actually work (and why AI needs them)

What it covers

Inside a graphics card are 2,048 tiny workers; the processor next to it has 8 big ones. A chatbot writes by guessing the next word, and every guess is math on numbers it learned in training: printed one per page, a big model's numbers would wrap around the Earth. On our own PC, the same chunk of AI math took the processor 10 seconds and the graphics card 1. Capp explains why with a classroom: 8 math professors (each juggling 16 problems) against a stadium of 2,048 students, a teacher calling one step to a whole row (that's lockstep), worksheets close by (the card's own 16 GB of memory), and the catch: when half a row needs a different step, they take turns. Then why AI's demand for chips and memory is a big part of why gaming cards cost more right now.

Prompting

What's a prompt, really? It's a coffee order

What it covers

Capp told Drip his prompts were too short. A prompt is whatever you type to the AI, and it's most of what the AI knows about the job. It's like ordering coffee: say "a coffee" and you get a coffee, say "a large oat latte" and you get the one you meant. The four things Drip adds now: the task, who it's for, an example (one good one often beats a paragraph) and the shape (a list, three lines, a table). Then a made-up before and after (a text to the boss), a trick for when you're stuck (ask it to ask you three questions first), and fixing one part in the same chat instead of starting over. Anthropic's own prompting guide gives the same core advice: be clear and direct, give context, use examples and say the format you want.

Seven legal and money risks for an app built with AI, and a prompt to check them

What it covers

A receipt prints seven risks an app built with AI can run into before its first sale, each one a real maximum or a real case, because some rules count per violation, per email or per text. In the US: COPPA (once you know a user is under 13, get a parent's OK or delete the data; the FTC can seek up to $53,088 per violation), CAN-SPAM (promo emails need an unsubscribe link and a mailing address; up to $53,088 per email if the government sues and a court sets it) and the TCPA (marketing texts can need written consent, and a person can sue for $500 per text without it).     prompt
    Audit this app for legal and money risks. List what fails first, and fix only after I say yes. Check that:

Claude Code

Claude can animate now: 12 motion graphics, all code

What it covers

12 motion graphics, each one playing on its own slide: words that slam in on the beat, 608 dots that turn into Drip, one shape that becomes a dashboard, goo that bounces, a word tunnel, a coverflow, a chat that gets stamped, a bar chart race, a shelf where books vanish, a balance scale, a data center that draws itself, and a zoom from a chip to Manhattan. Claude Opus 5.5 built all of them in code in Claude Code, and each took a few rounds of fixes. The prompts below describe each motion so you can make your own.

Everything we mentioned

  • Claude Code

    tool

    Where Claude built all 12, as code.

  • Remotion

    tool

    Turns the code into video, one frame at a time.

  • Our video on how these get made

    video

    Why every frame is a program, and why it takes more than one prompt.

The prompt for each one

Prompts to try. They describe each motion (every state, the springs, the loop) the way we asked Claude for them; expect a few rounds of fixes, the way we needed them.

  • 01 Words → slam

    prompt
    Make a 6-second looping motion graphic in code (HTML/SVG or Remotion, 30 fps, every frame a pure function of time). Dark background. Four words stacked: EVERY / FRAME / IS / CODE. They slam in one per beat, every 0.32 s: each word starts at 3x scale and a 12° tilt and springs to 1x with a little overshoot, with a quick orange glow on impact and a ring that pulses out from the centre. Last word orange, the rest cream. After the last word, the whole stack punches once and an orange underline sweeps in. Fade out in the last half second so it loops cleanly.

  • 02 Dots → Drip

    prompt
    Make a 6-second looping particle animation in SVG. About 600 dots start scattered and drifting. Give every dot a target point on the outline of a simple character (a coffee cup with two dot eyes, a small mouth and wavy steam above it). From 0.45 s each dot springs to its target with a small random delay, so the character assembles in a wave; hold it breathing slightly; at 4.5 s every dot springs back to where it started. Use one spring function (damped, closed form) for every move. Dark background, cream dots, orange steam.

  • 03 Button → dashboard

    prompt
    Make a 6-second looping 'one shape, never cut' animation. A single rounded rectangle changes state on springs, never swapping for a new element: a dot, then an orange 'Render ▶' button, a drawn cursor clicks it, it shrinks into a cream circle with a spinning loader ring, turns orange with a check mark drawing in, then grows into a white dashboard card with a number counting up to 12,480 and six bars growing in. Then it shrinks back to a dot and disappears. Name every state with its size, radius and colour, and animate position, size, corner radius and colour on springs.

  • 04 Blobs → bounce

    prompt
    Make a 6-second looping SVG animation using a gooey filter (Gaussian blur plus a color matrix alpha threshold). Seven orange blobs orbit the centre, then spring inward and melt into one ball. The ball drops and bounces three or four times with decaying height, squashing on each landing and stretching as it rises, with a soft shadow that grows as it lands. At 4.5 s it splits back into the seven orbiting blobs. Light paper background, a small caption: SQUASH · STRETCH · SPRINGS.

  • 05 Words → tunnel

    prompt
    Make a 6-second seamless loop: a z-tunnel. Streaks of light fly outward from the centre (speeds chosen so the loop is seamless), cream with every fifth one orange. Every 1.5 s a word flies from far away straight at the camera, scaling up exponentially and fading as it passes: SPRINGS, SEEK(t), BEATS, CODE. (last one orange). Dark background.

  • 06 Cards → coverflow

    prompt
    Make a 6-second looping coverflow of 7 portrait cards (use your own screenshots or frames). The centre card faces the camera; the others rotate away in 3D (rotateY about 44°) and shrink as they move out. Every 6/7 s the row springs one card to the left, wrapping around so the loop is seamless. Light paper background, soft shadows, a small caption under it.

  • 07 Chat → stamp

    prompt
    Make a 6-second looping chat animation. A user's orange bubble types out 'Is this book real?' next to a small avatar. Three typing dots pulse on the left, then a grey reply types out 'Yes! It's a bestseller.' A red rubber stamp slams down on top, reading IT ISN'T., starting at 2.5x scale and springing to 1x, while the whole card shakes briefly. Fade out and loop. Dark background, label it MADE-UP EXAMPLE.

  • 08 Bars → race

    prompt
